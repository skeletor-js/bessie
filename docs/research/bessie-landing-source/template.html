Bessie

A herd does not tell you when one of them has stopped. Six agents are running, two are waiting on an answer, and you will not know which until you get round to that tab. Bessie puts the whole herd in one window and pushes whoever needs you to the top.

Step two · find the one that stopped

Know who needs you without reading.

Bessie never uses colour to tell you what is happening — brightness does it. Whoever is waiting on you is the brightest thing on the screen, and everything else fades back in order. After a day you stop reading the rail and start glancing at it.

Step five · leave the rest alone

Closed, it still counts.

Close the window and the cow stays in your menu bar with the number waiting on you. Answer from the drop-down, or jump straight into the pane. Your agents carry on either way.

What it will not do

It never owns your work.

Herdr runs your agents. Bessie is only the window onto them — it starts nothing, moves nothing, and cannot lose a session. Close it, delete it, go back to the terminal: everything is still running where you left it.

You see the keystroke before you send itApproving sends 1 ↵ to bessie / dev / theme — nothing else is typed, and Bessie tells you that first.
You can check where a status came fromEvery reading shows its source — the agent itself, the process, or a guess from the screen — so you know how much to trust it.
It tells you what it cannot doPausing needs Herdr or the agent to support it. Where neither does, Bessie warns you instead of pretending it worked.
It does not guessA pane it cannot read is marked unknown — never quietly filed as idle so the screen looks tidy.

The herd keeps grazing whether or not you are watching. Bessie only decides how much of it you can see at once.

Point it at the Herdr you already run.

Nothing to migrate and nothing to restart. Install Bessie, attach it, and the sessions you have open show up as they are. Takes about a minute.
